About Me

At ten-years-old, I started to understand that innovation can begin from recognizing a problem that affects someone you care about. My mother experienced frequent nosebleeds, so I created a “bloody nose box” by modifying a tissue box with openings on the sides so that she could easily access tissues, plastic bags for ice, and cotton balls when a nosebleed began. Although the solution I created was a simple one, I identified an issue, determined what was needed, and created something to fix it sparked my interest in improving human health.

As I progressed through high school, this interest continued developing into a curiosity about biological mechanisms underlying human health. Through my classes, I was able to grow an understanding for different biological processes and human characteristics. I became fascinated with my biology classes, seeing how genetics and cellular levels could produce effects in the human body. I started seeing biology as more than a class, but as a framework for understanding why certain health problems arise and how they could be addressed.

Also, my interest in human health and my swimming background led me to become a lifeguard. This allowed me to understand that the human body needs to be protected and has varying levels of vulnerability. These experiences helped strengthen my desire to learn about the mechanisms that regulate human health.

I now attend the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill as a Biology major and Chemistry minor on the pre-medical track. Through my biology coursework, I intend to gain a deeper understanding of the cellular, molecular, and physiological processes that regulate human health. Also, at UNC, I plan to pursue undergraduate research to investigate biological questions and develop my laboratory skills to contribute to scientific discovery. By engaging in research, I hope to learn how scientists take an unanswered question and generate evidence for the betterment of medical practice.

As I pursue my career as a physician, I intend to carry my scientific curiosity into patient care. Practicing medicine requires more than recognizing symptoms but understanding the biological processes behind them and using my knowledge for determining treatment. Ultimately, my interest in Biology stems from that same inclination that led me to create a simple solution for my mother at ten-years-old; when I encounter a problem affecting human health, I want to understand why this occurs and use that understanding to find a meaningful solution.
